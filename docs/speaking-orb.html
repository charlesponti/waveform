When Thames talks, it gets its own orb

Today the reply reads like any chat thread. These three options give the voice a centre of gravity: an orb that moves with the speech and captions that appear as each word is spoken. All three play the same reply, so you can compare them directly.

Option A

Stage. Orb on top, captions beneath

The orb sits in the upper middle. Words appear below it as they are spoken and the older ones dim and drift up, so you can glance back at what was just said.

Waiting for a connection

Motion
Dots swell with the voice and breathe in the pauses between sentences. New words fade in over 0.35 s; older words settle to 42% opacity.
Haptics
One soft tick when the first word lands. Nothing per word, it would buzz. A light tap on stop.
Reduce Motion
The sphere stops turning and does not swell. Loudness shows as brightness. Words appear without fading.
No service
Orb dims, a small wifi-off icon and one quiet line. Captions already received stay.
Trade-off
Closest to what was asked. The orb is smaller than in B, but you keep a readable transcript.
Option B

Lantern. Captions live inside the orb

One big orb fills the middle and the current phrase is set inside it, one phrase at a time. When the phrase ends, the next replaces it.

Waiting for a connection

Motion
The orb is the whole screen, so it moves more gently. Phrases cross-fade; the active word is full strength.
Haptics
One soft tick at the first word. A very light tick on each new phrase would be possible; held back until tried on the phone.
Reduce Motion
Static sphere that brightens with the voice. Phrases swap without a fade.
No service
Orb dims, the current phrase stays in place.
Trade-off
Strongest identity, but the text sits on the dots, so contrast needs care. No history on screen; it needs a way to scroll back.
Option C

Subtitle. A glow with film-style captions

A smaller orb sits in a soft field of light that pulses with the voice. Captions run as two-line subtitles just above the dock.

Waiting for a connection

Motion
The field behind the orb scales 1.0 to 1.12 with loudness. Subtitles replace each other every phrase, never more than six words.
Haptics
One soft tick at the first word. A light tap on stop.
Reduce Motion
The field holds still and only changes brightness.
No service
Field fades out, the subtitle stays.
Trade-off
Easiest to read and the least intrusive. The orb is the smallest of the three, so it feels the least like a character.

Nothing starts by itself. After the reply ends the orb settles and the dock offers the mic, it never starts listening on its own. The stop button in the dock ends the reply early.

My pick

Option A. It is the closest to what you described, and it is the only one that keeps a readable trail of what was said. That matters when someone looks away and wants to catch up.

I would steal one idea from C: the soft field behind the orb, so A's orb feels bigger without taking more room.
